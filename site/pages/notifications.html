Notifications and email

ByteBench tells you about the things that involve you through a live bell in the top bar, optional emails and digests, browser push notifications and, if you use Slack, direct messages, and every kind can be tuned from one preferences page.

In-app notifications

The bell in the top bar shows a live unread count and a dropdown with the latest 10 notifications. Click one to mark it read and open the item, or use Mark all read. The full list is at /notifications, with All and Unread tabs and paging.

  • The person who acted is never notified, and each user gets at most one notification per event. Assignment and mention take priority over the watcher notification.
  • Recipients are always restricted to people who can see the project, so a notification never leaks a private item.
  • Edited descriptions notify only users who are newly mentioned. Edited comments notify nobody.

Live updates

Boards, issue lists, issue and pull request pages, the dashboard activity feed, the notifications page and the bell update on their own through ByteBench's in-process event hub. Two browsers on the same board see a card move without reloading. A refresh replaces only the loaded data, so text you are typing in a description, title or comment is not touched.

The event hub lives inside one server process. A deployment with several ByteBench servers behind a load balancer would need a shared backplane for live updates; see production setup.

Watching and muting

  • You watch an issue automatically when you report it, are assigned to it or comment on it. You watch a pull request when you author it, review it, are a reviewer or comment on it. The Watch / Watching button on the issue and pull request pages toggles it.
  • Mute (next to Watching) keeps the watch but stops watcher notifications. Direct notifications still arrive: assigned to you, mentioned, review requested, reviews of your own pull request.
  • Unwatching is not the same as muting. ByteBench auto-watches you again when you comment, are assigned or review, so an unwatch may not stick, while a mute does. Unwatching also clears the mute.

Mentions and autocomplete

Type @ and a letter in any Markdown editor (issue description, new issue, comments, pull request description and comments, inline diff comments) to get up to 8 people who can view the project. Each row shows the display name, @username and avatar colour. Typing # and a number or text suggests issues, and a project key like WEB- suggests that project's issues. Pick with the arrow keys and Enter or Tab, or the mouse; Esc closes the list.

  • Mentions match existing usernames case-insensitively and are ignored inside code spans and code blocks. Known users render highlighted.
  • Suggestions come only from people who can view the project (site admins, members, and everyone if the project is Internal). The AI review bot is never suggested.
  • Lookups run as you and return nothing for a project you cannot view.

Notification preferences

Open /account/notifications (linked from your profile and the bell dropdown). For each group, choose In-app + email, In-app only or Off. A master switch turns all notification emails off. "Don't notify me about my own actions" is shown and is always on.

When your Slack account is linked and the server has a Slack bot token, a switch Also send my notifications as Slack direct messages appears here. See Slack direct messages.

Digest emails

A digest is a summary email at a time that suits you. On /account/notifications, under Digest email, choose Off (the default), Daily or Weekly, the hour, the weekday for weekly digests and your time zone (an IANA name such as Europe/Berlin; the page suggests your browser's zone if you have not picked one), then Save digest settings. The page shows when the next digest goes out.

A digest contains, for projects you can still view when it is sent:

  • Unread notifications grouped by project: the count and the newest five, with links.
  • Unread mentions.
  • Issues assigned to you that are overdue or due within 7 days.
  • Open pull requests where you are a requested reviewer and have not reviewed yet.
  • Your own open pull requests that are ready to merge (approved, nothing blocking, no pending or failed check) or have a failing check.

If there is nothing to report, no email is sent. The digest comes on top of your other emails and replaces none of them. It follows your preferences: groups set to Off are left out, watcher notifications about muted issues and pull requests are left out, and turning off Send me notification emails (or the all-emails unsubscribe link) stops digests too.

  • Times follow your zone across daylight saving changes. An hour that does not exist that day (clocks go forward) moves to the first valid time after it; an hour that happens twice (clocks go back) uses the first one.
  • Send me a preview now builds and sends the digest right away, at most three times an hour. It does not change the schedule.
  • Every digest has a one-click unsubscribe link (also as List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers) to /account/digest/unsubscribe. It is signed for your account, valid 90 days and needs no sign-in; it turns off digests only.
  • A failed send is retried on the next few polls (up to three times), then skipped until the next period. The server keeps a small log of digest runs with counts only, for 30 days.

Browser push notifications

ByteBench can show notifications from your browser even when no ByteBench tab is open. Under Browser push on /account/notifications, click Enable browser notifications. Your browser asks for permission only after that click. Each browser (or phone) you enable appears in the list of subscribed browsers, at most 10, and you can remove any of them. Turn off for this browser unsubscribes the browser you are using.

The switch Send notifications to my subscribed browsers and the group checkboxes choose what is pushed. By default: assigned to me, mentions and review requests. Groups set to Off above and muted items are never pushed. Clicking a notification opens the item, focusing an open ByteBench tab when there is one.

  • Pushes are sent in the background, so a slow push service never slows down the page that caused the notification.
  • When your browser replaces a subscription on its own (it rotates or expires), the service worker subscribes again and swaps it on the server without you doing anything, as long as you are still signed in.
  • If a browser's subscription has expired, or delivery fails five times in a row, it is removed. If a push service asks ByteBench to slow down, that browser is skipped for the time it asks.
  • When the browser does not support push, you blocked notifications for the site, or the site is not served over HTTPS, the page says so instead of showing the button.
  • iPhone and iPad: push works only from the installed web app (iOS 16.4 or later). In Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, open ByteBench from the Home Screen and enable notifications there.

What a push contains

A push message is end-to-end encrypted for your browser (Web Push with aes128gcm and VAPID), but it still passes through your browser vendor's push service, and lock screens show it. So it carries only the item's title, one short line of at most 120 characters (for example Alice Chen commented on) and a link to the item on this site. Comment text and issue or pull request descriptions are never included. The link is always a path on this ByteBench site.

Browser push setup (administrators)

Browser push is on by default and needs no keys: ByteBench creates its VAPID key pair the first time it is needed and stores the private key encrypted with Data Protection (back up the Data Protection key ring as for other secrets). Browsers only allow push on HTTPS sites (and localhost). The server must be able to reach the browser vendors' push services over HTTPS.

Subscription addresses come from browsers, so ByteBench treats them like webhook URLs: only https, no credentials or fragment, never a loopback, private, link-local or cloud metadata address (checked again when connecting, so DNS changes cannot get around it), no redirects, and a 10 second timeout.

A real push needs a browser and its vendor's push service, so the automated tests use a local fake push service that decrypts each message the way a browser does.

Email setup (administrators)

Email is off by default. While Email:Enabled is false nothing is queued and pending notification emails are dropped, so switching it on does not send a backlog.

Never put SMTP credentials in appsettings.json. Use user-secrets in development and environment variables in production:

# production (Docker, systemd)
Email__Enabled=true
Email__Sender=Smtp
Email__Smtp__Host=smtp.example.com
Email__Smtp__Port=587
Email__Smtp__User=bytebench@example.com
Email__Smtp__Password=<from your secret store>
Email__From=ByteBench <bytebench@example.com>
Email__BaseUrl=https://bytebench.example.com

# development
dotnet user-secrets set "Email:Enabled" "true"
dotnet user-secrets set "Email:Sender" "Smtp"

How email is sent

  • Emails go on an in-memory queue and a background worker sends them, so a page never waits on SMTP. A failed send is retried 3 times (after 2, 10 and 30 seconds), then logged and dropped. Log lines mask addresses (b***@example.com).
  • A second worker turns pending notifications into emails whenever notifications change and every 30 seconds. It skips anything you already read in the app.
  • With a digest interval above 0, the first email goes out immediately and later ones are grouped.

What the email contains

Each email is built from the same notification the bell shows: who did it, what happened, the title and a link to the item. It also includes an excerpt of the comment or description (at most 1,000 characters). The HTML part renders the excerpt with the same safe Markdown as the site (raw HTML disabled), and the plain-text part quotes the Markdown source. Every other piece of user content is HTML-encoded.

Every email links to your preferences and carries a signed one-click unsubscribe link (valid 90 days, also sent as List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers) that switches off all notification emails for that user.

Replying to emails

Replying to a notification email does not add a comment to the issue or pull request; use the link in the email. Reply handling exists for the service desk, where requesters answer by email and replies above the marker line are added to the ticket.
